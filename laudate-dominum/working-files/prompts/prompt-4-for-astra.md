Claude here, relayed by the composer. The composer reviewed the finished motet and was right on two counts. At about 2½ minutes, it is far too short to be grand and magnificent. It is also almost all note-against-note. By my measurement, 80–90% of the attack points in our clauses were block chords, at only two or three attacks per bar. Most of that is my fault: my plan asked for "mostly declamatory and chordal" writing, and my Choir II set that example. So we are writing version 2 from scratch: a festive double-choir motet of about six minutes in real polychoral counterpoint. Version 1 is archived in laudate-dominum\v1\.

WHAT STAYS THE SAME
- Text: Psalm 116 with the Gloria Patri and Amen. Words may now be repeated freely, as in the period.
- Mode: F with one flat.
- Ranges: Cantus c'-f'', Altus f-bes', Tenor c-f', Bassus f,-d'. Each choir must be complete on its own, and its Bassus is a real bass.
- All the style rules from the first message: leaps, dissonance treatment, no parallel fifths or octaves, underlay and musica ficta written with \ficta.
- All the notation rules: absolute nederlands pitches, no \relative, no double angle brackets, one bar per line ending "| % <bar>", and the same variable names (cIcantus ... cIbassusText).

WHAT CHANGES
- 130 bars. Cut C 4/2 in bars 1-64 and 85-130. Tripla 3/1 in bars 65-84: three semibreves to the bar, faster, so every bar lasts the same time.
- Texture: points of imitation within each choir, antiphonal exchanges, a jubilant tripla Gloria, and four eight-voice tutti sections.

JOINS
- In 4/2, the rule is the same as before. The outgoing choir arrives on the listed chord on beat 1, holds it for one semibreve, then rests. The incoming choir sings only notes of that chord in beats 1-2, or rests. At the last bar of a tutti, the choir that carries on counts as incoming.
- In tripla, there is no overlap. The outgoing choir ends on its chord as a breve (beats 1-2), then r1. The incoming choir rests through beats 1-2 and enters on beat 3 or at the next bar.
- In a tutti, the leading composer writes a complete four-voice choir, and the other composer adds theirs afterwards. You lead sections 4 and 9 this round. I lead sections 7, 12 and 14. For those, next round I'll give you my Choir II and a fixed Bassus I, and you'll add Cantus I, Altus I and Tenor I.

YOUR PART THIS ROUND

Sec 1, bars 1-11, "Laudate Dominum omnes gentes": the exordium.
- Invent a soggetto for "Laudate Dominum". It will come back: Choir II answers with it in section 2, and the final Amen (section 14) is built on it. Make it strong and singable: open with a long note, include one rising leap of a 4th or 5th, then move mostly by step, 5-8 notes over about two to three bars.
- All four voices enter with it in turn, one to two bars apart, with real or tonal answers at the 4th or 5th. Use any order.
- Then set "omnes gentes" in livelier rhythm: a crotchet melisma, syncopation.
- Cadence: an F-major chord on beat 1 of bar 11, as a semibreve, then r1. A suspension leading into it is welcome.

Sec 3a, bars 20-22, "omnes gentes": an exclamation.
- Bar 20, beats 1-2: only C, E or G, or rests (Choir II is holding C).
- Chordal but lively. The voices may be staggered by a minim.
- End on an F chord on beat 1 of bar 22, as a semibreve, then r1.

Sec 4, bars 24-31, TUTTI (you lead), "Laudate Dominum omnes gentes": the grand restatement.
- Bar 24, beats 1-2: only B-flat, D or F, or rests (Choir II is holding B-flat).

  - keep the harmony clear, with root-position and 6/3 chords;
  - keep Cantus I mostly below e''.
- The soggetto in long notes in one voice would be splendid here.
- Bar 31, beats 1-2: an F-major chord (the last chord of the tutti). Section 5 may begin on beat 3.

Sec 5, bars 31-44, "Quoniam confirmata est super nos misericordia eius": the contrast section, lighter and more intimate.

Sec 8, tripla, chordal and dancing.

TEXTURE TARGETS (I'll measure them by script)
- Sections 1 and 5: block chords (moments when every singing voice attacks at once) at no more than 40% of attack points, and at least four attacks per bar on average across the choir.
- Sections 3a, 4, 8, 9 and 11: chordal writing is right here, but keep block chords to no more than 75%. Vary the note lengths and use dotted rhythms, rests and suspensions.
- Use the whole rhythmic vocabulary:
  - breves, semibreves and minims;
  - a dotted minim + crotchet;
  - crotchet runs of 2-8 notes in melismas (stepwise, in duple time only);
  - syncopated suspensions in mid-phrase;
  - rests inside voices.
